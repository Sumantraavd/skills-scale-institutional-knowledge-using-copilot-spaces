# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

Welcome to the OctoAcme project management process documentation. These guides describe a customer-first, iterative, and data-informed approach to delivering product features, services, and integrations. They establish clear ownership, align stakeholders around measurable outcomes, manage risks and dependencies, and provide a shared source of truth for planning and execution.

OctoAcme manages work through a lifecycle that begins with project initiation and continues through planning, execution, release, and retrospective improvement. Initiation confirms the business need, success metrics, stakeholders, timeline, risks, and resources. Planning turns an approved initiative into a prioritized backlog with acceptance criteria, estimates, dependencies, a Definition of Done, and a release plan. During execution, teams use project boards, small pull requests, regular delivery ceremonies, progress metrics, and blocker escalation to maintain visibility and momentum.

The process defines complementary roles for Project Managers, Product Managers, developers, QA and testing contributors, and stakeholders. Project Managers coordinate delivery, schedules, risks, resources, and communications; Product Managers own product outcomes, prioritization, and success measures; developers build and test maintainable solutions; QA validates quality and acceptance criteria; and stakeholders provide input and approvals. Communication is supported by standups, weekly delivery or PM and Product Manager syncs, milestone reviews, stakeholder updates, shared project artifacts, and escalation paths from the team to the PM, Product Lead, and sponsor when needed.

Quality is built into the workflow through acceptance criteria, code review, automated tests, linting, security scanning, integration and end-to-end testing, manual QA when appropriate, and release verification. Releases require passing CI and security checks, completed acceptance criteria, release notes, smoke tests, and a rollback or mitigation plan. After sprints, releases, milestones, and incidents, retrospectives capture learnings and turn them into a small number of owned, time-bound improvements, enabling continuous refinement of the process.
